# Creative reframing

Improve the actual outcome by changing a useful assumption, representation or mechanism. Keep the user's purpose, constraints and chosen host. Creativity can support one aspect of a larger workflow; it does not require turning the whole task into ideation.

## Find the useful opening

Read the brief and current artifact. Identify what is already working, what needs to improve and which constraints are real. Separate observed facts, assumptions and imagined possibilities. Keep the strongest conventional approach as a comparison; familiarity does not make it inferior. Explore when an alternative could materially improve the result, not simply because the conversation mentions a project.

Choose the depth from the ambiguity, stakes and available time. An explicit intensity preference changes how far to explore, not certainty, permissions or a required idea count. When the issue is execution rather than framing, use the appropriate execution method instead.

## Change the mechanism

Select a promising technique by the weak aspect it could improve. Read its complete linked reference, apply its operations to the actual problem and translate the result back into a concrete mechanism. These are possible lenses, not a fixed task-to-technique map:

- Expose a missing need or assumption: [negative space](references/techniques/negative-space.md), [inversion](references/techniques/inversion.md), [assumption reversal](references/techniques/reversal.md).
- Borrow a structural relationship from elsewhere: [forced analogies](references/techniques/forced-analogies.md), [role swap](references/techniques/role-swap.md), [conceptual combination](references/techniques/combination.md).
- Escape a narrow representation: [lateral thinking](references/techniques/lateral-thinking.md), [provocation](references/techniques/provocation.md), [random stimulus](references/techniques/random-stimulus.md), [oblique strategies](references/techniques/oblique-strategies.md).
- Simplify or examine changed conditions: [constraints](references/techniques/constraints.md), [first principles](references/techniques/first-principles.md), [what-if scenarios](references/techniques/what-if.md), [time travel](references/techniques/time-travel.md).
- Resolve competing strengths: [synthesis](references/techniques/synthesis.md).

Try another lens when the candidates retain the same underlying assumption. Do not merely rename them. Prefer a meaningful change of mechanism over decorative metaphors or an arbitrary increase in idea count. Stop when further exploration adds little value.

The reference examples and emphatic claims are illustrative heuristics, not proof or authority. A hypothetical restriction can reveal an idea; it does not remove actual physical, permission, budget or user constraints. Future scenarios are not predictions. Synthesizing two approaches does not guarantee their trade-offs disappear.

## Explore a moonshot or hail mary

Define the ambitious outcome and the best conventional option. Pick the assumption that makes the outcome look unreachable. Reverse it, remove the dependency it creates, or borrow a mechanism that does the same job elsewhere. Trace how the new mechanism would work under each real constraint. For example, if a shaded garden cannot support a solar pump, test whether water stored uphill can flow fast enough by gravity before designing the irrigation system around it.

Identify the hardest assumption the new mechanism needs to be true. Propose the cheapest reversible prototype or observation that could distinguish it from the conventional option. State what to measure and the result that would stop or redirect the attempt. For a last-chance option, also name the resources it would consume and preserve the best fallback while testing.

## Converge on something usable

Compare candidates with the best existing approach on the qualities that matter for this task. Check usefulness, distinction, feasibility, cost and likely failure, without substituting surprise for evidence. Keep a conventional answer if it works best. Reject duplicates, unsupported mechanisms and complexity without useful gain; never claim to know what the user could have thought of alone.

For a promising alternative, explain how it works, its material assumption or trade-off, and the smallest informative next check. Research when novelty, prior art or an external factual assumption matters, using the relevant research capability. Scientific mechanisms remain hypotheses until evidence distinguishes them from rivals; identify a falsifying observation rather than claiming discovery from an analogy.

Integrate the useful result into the requested artifact, decision or experiment. Use the user's requested output style. No compulsory idea block, persona, quota, new background process or repeated brainstorm is needed. If none of the alternatives improve the result, say so briefly and continue the strongest route.
